HOW TO TRANSCRIBE THESE RECORDINGS
==================================

This folder has:

  audio/V001.wav, ...      5-minute stretches of home audio from LENA recorders
  templates/V001.csv, ...  one table per recording, for you to fill in

You're writing down every word spoken in each recording, from scratch. There
is no computer transcript to correct, on purpose: we'll use your transcripts
to measure how accurate the computer transcripts are.


WHAT TO DO
----------

1. Open the recording in a player that shows the time, such as Audacity
   or VLC, and its template in Excel or another spreadsheet.

2. Each row of the template is one stretch of the recording (a "span"),
   from "start" to "end" (minutes:seconds). The rows cover the whole
   recording with no gaps. Listen to each span, replaying as often as you
   need. Headphones help a lot.

3. Fill in these columns for EVERY row:

   status   speech   you heard at least one word
            none     no words at all (silence, noise, crying, babbling)
            unclear  someone is talking, but you can't make out any word

   speech   every word said by a person in the room: adults and children

   media    every word from a TV, radio, phone, video, or talking toy

   notes    anything else (optional)

4. Don't add, delete, or reorder rows.

5. Save the file as CSV (in Excel: File > Save As > "CSV UTF-8"), with
   your initials added to the name: V001_AB.csv. Put finished files in
   the "returned" folder next to this one.


THE LENA HINT
-------------

The lena_hint column says what the LENA software thinks is in that span,
for example "LENA heard: woman 3.2s" or "LENA: no speech". It's a guess and
is often wrong in both directions. Use it as a heads-up about where to listen
closely, and never as a reason to skip listening: every row needs a status,
including rows where LENA heard nothing.


HOW TO WRITE WHAT YOU HEAR
--------------------------

- Write exactly what was said, including false starts, repeats, and
  mistakes ("we we went to the- to the store"). Don't fix grammar.

- A word that starts in one span and ends in the next goes in the span
  where it started.

- A word you can't make out: write xxx in its place, one xxx for each
  stretch you can't make out ("can you get the xxx from the kitchen").
  Don't guess from context. If you can't make out any word in the span,
  set status to unclear instead.

- Fillers and short responses: write them as heard (um, uh, mm-hmm,
  uh-oh, shh).

- Babbling, crying, laughing, coughing, and singing without words: don't
  write them as words. If you want to note them, put them in square
  brackets: [babble], [cry], [laugh]. Anything in square brackets is
  ignored when we score.

- A child's word counts only when you can recognize a real word ("mama",
  "ball", "uh-oh"). Babble that only sounds word-like is [babble].

- Names: write them as you hear them.

- Spell normally and write numbers as words ("two", not "2"). Don't
  write speaker labels like "Mom:" in the text.

- Don't use any transcription service, captioning tool, or AI tool, and
  don't look at other people's transcripts of the same recording. Some
  recordings go to two people on purpose, to check how often people agree.


QUESTIONS
---------

If something comes up that these instructions don't cover, write it in the
notes column, do what seems most sensible, and ask your supervisor so that
everyone handles it the same way.
